    How to read the plot

    Each cell is one split of the moral weight. Its colour lists every outcome that can be sustained there: (A,A), (A,B), (B,A), (B,B), or any combination, or none.

    The moral weight wij is how much player i cares about player j's value judgment. With s(x) = 2x / (2x + 1), the paper sets

    w11 = W·s(Ine)·s(Ind),   w21 = W·s(Ine)·(1−s(Ind)),   w12 = W·(1−s(Ine))·(1−s(Ind)),   w22 = W·(1−s(Ine))·s(Ind).
    • Inequality sets player 1's share of projecting moral weight (the weight all players place on player 1's judgments): (w11 + w21) / W = s(Ine). At 0 the shares are equal; positive values favour player 1.
    • Individualism sets the share of all moral weight that players place on their own judgments: (w11 + w22) / W = s(Ind). At 0 this share is one half.

    SPNE evaluates an off-path subgame by its worst equilibrium for the deviator. RTC SPNE keeps the on-path outcome in a subgame whenever it is still an equilibrium there.
